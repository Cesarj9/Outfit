PATCH NOTES

What's new in Pokelike

v3.7.1
Clearer relics, classic options and fixes
2026-11-14
CHANGES
Every relic has a new, clearer description: a short line on the card that says what it does, and the "i" for the details. In all 8 languages.
A shiny duplicate from a Legendary Egg now gives 15 Comet Shards instead of 20.
Settings: "Classic animations" brings back every battle animation from before 3.7, and "Classic text font" shows relic, trait and ability text in the old font.
The win screens have a Home button, and they now show the Gym badge or Hardcore reward you just earned.
The Battle Tower shows its Hardcore rewards only in Hardcore.
FIXES
A Pokémon now gets the Mega Stone it can use: Gardevoir no longer gets Galladite.
Starf Berry also gave its random boosts to enemies.
Macho Brace's second hit ignored damage relics and traits.
Odd Incense replayed the same Psychic splash every turn.
Some relic names showed an old name in other languages, like King's Rock in Spanish.
v3.7
Relics, Gym badges and a Hardcore Battle Tower
2026-11-13
RELICS
Passive items are now called Relics, everywhere in the game.
127 new Relics. 49 old ones leave the pool (runs that already hold one keep it), and every Relic has a shorter, clearer description.
Before the first map you pick 1 of 3 starting Relics made for your starter's type.
The first card on each Relic screen builds on what you already hold. The other two are random.
Z-Crystals: hold 3 Relics for one type while its trait is active, and that type's Z-Crystal shows up. There is one for every type.
Charm Relics (Shiny Hunter, Legend Lure, Oval Charm) no longer take a Relic slot. Once unlocked, Shiny Hunter joins every run with Relics as a second starting Relic.
Relic cards show icons for the traits they work with, and a SHINY or LEGENDARY tag when they need one.
New one-use Relics: Dowsing Machine gives two random Relics, Rotom Catalog lets you pick a starting Relic for any type on your team. Eevee starters can take Eevium Z: when Eevee evolves, you pick 1 of 3 Relics of its new type.
GYM CHALLENGE
Challenge Mode is now the Gym Challenge, with a badge case of 8 badges.
Win a run to earn the next badge. Each badge level adds one rule on top of the ones before: trainer battles get type traits, wild Pokémon are 3 levels higher, gym leaders bring 2 shinies, a cursed Iron Ball, enemies are 10% higher level, trainers 5 more levels, and a second Champion waits at the end.
Each badge pays a reward once, the first time, from 2,500₽ up to a Shiny Legendary Egg (see the badge case). Every clear also pays 1,000₽, plus 100₽ per badge level, and 2,000₽ with two Champions.
BATTLE TOWER
The Battle Tower has Normal and Hardcore. Normal plays with Relics: a starting Relic and one after every trainer fight. Hardcore is the tower as it was.
Each mode unlocks its own stages. A Hardcore clear also unlocks the next stage in Normal, and every clear from before this patch counts as Hardcore.
Clearing a stage on Hardcore pays once: Kanto 2,000₽, Johto 1 Comet Shard, Hoenn 4,000₽, Sinnoh 2 Comet Shards, Unova 8,000₽, Kalos 1 Mega Token, ORAS 3 Aurora Eggs and 10,000₽. Stages you already cleared pay out the next time you open the tower.
Aurora Eggs always hatch an Aurora Pokémon of a line you don't have as an Aurora yet. Hatch them from the ORAS card.
MEGA EVOLUTION
Mega Tokens work differently: each one adds 5 MVP wins toward the Mega Stone of your choice, on every tier including the 50-win stones. They don't count toward the MVP Pokédollar milestones.
The Volcano Badge now gives 2 Mega Tokens, worth the same 10 MVP wins as before.
BATTLES
New animations for most attacks, every trait and 18 legendary signature moves. Settings has a switch to bring back the classic trait animations.
Traits show their own icons, with a medal for the tier, in battle and on the map.
Cards show a short line for each trait and ability. Hover or tap it to read the full text.
OTHER CHANGES
The Nuzlocke Randomizer weekly is back: permadeath, every Pokémon a random species, one catch per map. Five difficulties, Gen 1 to Gen 5, paying 2,000₽ to 10,000₽.
SpireMon takes a break and comes back with Gen 2. Your cards stay in your save.
The PC in the top bar has a Missing button that lists the Pokémon lines you don't have yet.
On phones, the PC, Pokédex, Pokésort and the other big windows leave a small gap at the edges, so you can still see the game behind them.
New achievement: Kanto Classics. Beat Gen 1 with Venusaur, Charizard and Blastoise on your team.
A Charizard holding Charizardite X now counts as a Dragon for the team-type achievements.
FIXES
Volcanion's signature move always used Water. It now picks Fire or Water, whichever hits harder.
On phones, the top of the Relic replace screen could not be reached.

v3.6
Kalos: the Gen 6 story, Halloween Part 2 and Japanese
2026-10-30
KALOS STORY
The Gen 6 story is here: nine maps across Kalos, from Santalune City to the Pokémon League. Chespin, Fennekin and Froakie are the starters, Viola to Wulfric run the gyms, and the Elite Four and Champion Diantha wait at the end. It unlocks once you have won any story.
Professor Sycamore (map 2) battles you with the three Kanto starters and gives you one of them if you win. On map 5, Korrina’s Mega Lucario takes on one Pokémon of your choice: win and she hands you a Mega Stone that fits your team, lose and it costs you nothing. Beat three Team Flare Grunts and Lysandre blocks your path.
One Master Ball on map 7: two of the six Kalos legendaries appear at random. Pick one and beat it to catch it. Xerneas, Yveltal and Zygarde each bring a once-per-run power for a boss fight: Aura of Life revives one of your Pokémon once, Aura of Death makes one enemy faint before the fight, and Aura of Order lets you set the order of both teams.
From map 2, some wild battles are hordes: three wild Pokémon at once, worth more levels.
Every Pokémon battles with its Ability. Mega Evolution is the region’s gimmick: from map 5, item nodes can offer a Mega Stone that fits your team, no unlock needed. Megas keep their normal Ability here. Charizard can also find Charizardite X, which is story-only for now: Mega Charizard X is Fire/Dragon and attacks physically with Dragon moves.
48 Kalos achievements, including Horde Breaker, David & Lucario, Kanto Revival, Mirror Match and the four Abandon challenges.
WEEKLY: HALLOWEEN PART 2
Two Kalos Challenge Runs: only Gen 6 Pokémon as starters, in catch nodes and as legendaries, every Pokémon battles with its Ability, and the bosses are the real Kalos gym leaders, the Elite Four and Champion Diantha.
Kalos (3,000₽): the run as it is. Lights Out (4,000₽): the same run in the dark. You only see the path behind you and the next nodes you can pick, each under a flickering light.
Double Pokédollars Weekend: until the end of Sunday 1 November (Berlin time), Pokédollar rewards are doubled.
JAPANESE
Pokelike is now available in Japanese, including Pokémon, move and item names.
On your first visit the game now starts in your browser’s language if it is supported. A language you picked in Settings is always kept.
NEW & IMPROVED
The Hall of Fame PC is now just the PC and opens in its own window. It can filter by up to two types (the Pokédex can too), and you can mark favourites.
MVP milestones: 1, 10, 25, 50 and 100 MVP wins with an evolution line pay 250₽, 1,000₽, 2,500₽, 5,000₽ and 10,000₽. Claim them from the line’s Pokédex page.
30 new achievements, five for each story region: Frequent Visitor, Rainbow Team, Pure Types, Brute Force and Mind Over Matter.
A Mega whose move is already at the top tier can use a TM to switch its attack to its other type.
The Mega Stones collection has a new Event section: Mega Charizard X and Mega Mewtwo X are coming in a future event.
The “Trait Animations” setting is now “Trait & Ability Animations” and also covers Ability effects.

Finishing a run that is already in the Hall of Fame (an old save played to the end again) no longer pays or counts a second time.
Soul Link: restarting after a wipe only pays for maps beyond your best in that room.
SpireMon: resetting with R while a card choice was open could keep the cards.
Region achievements you had already earned could not be claimed during a run that turns achievements off.
Some Gen 3 Pokémon, such as Roserade, had no Ability.
Mega Sableye now attacks physically.
Achievement and item icons that showed a symbol instead of a picture now show the right sprite.
v3.5
Hoenn returns: the ORAS Battle Tower, 20 new Megas and Mega Hoenn with Wally
2026-10-16
ORAS BATTLE TOWER
A new Battle Tower stage opens once you have cleared Kalos: ORAS, nine maps across Hoenn. Treecko, Torchic and Mudkip are the starters, and you can catch any Gen 3 Pokémon plus every evolution line that has a Mega Stone.
Its bosses are Tabitha & Shelly, Aarune, Lisia, Courtney & Matt, Professors Oak & Birch, Wally, Steven, Maxie & Archie and Zinnia. Their Mega and Primal Pokémon fight with their Mega abilities and Mega typing, and legendaries in a Mega, Primal or special form use their signature moves. Both apply only in this tower: Kalos and the World Tournament are unchanged.
Two new achievements: Delta Champion (defeat Zinnia and clear Stage 7) and Birch’s Encore (win a Stage 7 run starting with Treecko, Torchic or Mudkip).
20 NEW MEGAS
20 new Mega Stones from Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, each with its own ability: Mega Sceptile, Swampert, Beedrill, Pidgeot, Audino, Sableye, Glalie, Camerupt, Sharpedo, Lopunny, Altaria, Slowbro, Steelix, Gallade, Salamence, Metagross and Diancie, plus Mega Rayquaza (Meteorite Shard) and Primal Kyogre and Groudon (Primal Blue and Red Orbs). Hover a stone in the Mega Stones collection to read what it does.
They unlock once you have cleared the ORAS Battle Tower, then through MVP wins like every other stone.
Primal Kyogre’s heavy rain makes Fire and Ground moves fail, Primal Groudon’s harsh sun makes Water moves fail, and Mega Rayquaza’s strong winds remove every weakness, resistance and immunity for the rest of the fight. While any of them is active, Drizzle, Drought, Sand Stream and Snow Warning fail.
A Kirlia holding Gardevoirite that evolves into Gallade now trades it for Galladite (if you own Galladite, or the run hands out stones), so a Ralts can Mega Evolve whichever way it goes.
WEEKLY: MEGA HOENN
This week’s weekly is a Gen 3 story Nuzlocke: one catch per map, and a Pokémon that faints is gone for good. Your starter is one of three random Hoenn Pokémon that can Mega Evolve, and it carries its Mega Stone from the first step. It Mega Evolves as soon as it reaches the right form, without its signature ability. Enemies are 2 levels lower than usual, and 4 lower after the fifth gym.
Three ways to play: Solo (2,000₽), with Wally (5,000₽), or Soul Link with a friend (5,000₽).
With Wally, a battle partner from the main games takes your friend’s place in a Soul Link, with no code and no server. Your starter is bound to his Ralts, and each catch to the next Pokémon that joins him. After every gym you beat, he takes on the same leader in the best order he can find. Whatever falls on either side takes its partner with it, and if his whole team falls, you both start over. When one of his Pokémon falls, a fresh one joins for your next catch: nine in all, Mega Gallade included.
Mega Weekend: until the end of Sunday 18 October (Berlin time), a winning Challenge run whose MVP is from a Mega line counts twice toward that line’s Mega Stone.
SOUL LINK
The level curve is now a flat step instead of a multiplier: every enemy is 1 level lower than usual, and 2 lower after the fifth gym. The old ×0.6 levels made it far too easy.
A Pokémon caught into a full team is now linked properly. Before, it arrived unlinked and the link could never take it.
Nincada no longer leaves a free Shedinja in a Soul Link run, since nothing may join your team outside a catch.
If the link server does not support the mode you chose, the lobby now says so instead of starting the wrong campaign.
ENDLESS
Creative is now part of Endless Normal: a saved Creative run moves to your Normal slot with its team, items, run buffs and maps cleared (if you also had a Normal run, the game asks which one to keep), your earlier Endless bests count toward Endless Normal, and Perpetual Motion (map 100) and Truly Endless (map 250) are now earned there. Achievements you already have stay. Endless Hardcore’s personal best starts fresh.
Late-game rewards now grow with the level curve. In Hardcore, Power Surge and Veteran Scout scale up the further you get (Power Surge gives +10 levels at map 5, about +29 at map 20 and +66 at map 50); in Normal, the Power Surge run buff rises to about +12 by map 50. Each card shows the real number.
New passive, Endless only: Safety Goggles. Poison deals 50% less damage to your Pokémon.
Settings → Auto-Skip → Endless Region Intro skips the animation at the start of each region, and the hover card now lists the stat boosts a Pokémon earned in the Underground.
A lost fight is saved to the cloud right away, and Continue checks the cloud first: a run that was lost or finished on another device can no longer be continued, and a tab still playing it stops.
NEW & IMPROVED
Item Guide: every passive, held and usable item in one searchable list. Open it from “Items” in the menu, or with the I key.
Evolutions can be stopped: press Stop (or B) while the silhouettes flash, and the game asks again at the next level-up, as in the main games. The button is hidden when Auto-Skip Evolutions is on.
Trades never offer the line you are trading away, or any of the last three lines you traded away. In Challenge and the Battle Tower, a shiny you trade away now comes back shiny.
The EV picker moves Pokémon that can’t take the chosen vitamin to the end, and hides lines that already have all 50 EVs (search still finds them).
Mutation progress now shows on the hover card, and the Griseous Orb list shows it instead of HP. Trait tooltips also show what the next tier gives.
17 Signature Items that shared a name with a regular item have their own names now, such as Dreamdust Wings (was Silver Powder), Pay Day Charm (Amulet Coin) and Hex Veil (Spell Tag). Their effects are unchanged.
Yache Berry now only raises the attacking stat its holder actually uses (or Defense, Speed or Sp. Def), so a boost never lands on a stat it doesn’t use.
SpireMon: Space now ends your turn, like E.
FIXES
Weather effects stuttered: their 30 fps limit actually ran at 20–30 fps with uneven frames, worst in Firefox and Safari. Weather now animates smoothly at your screen’s refresh rate (60 fps on most screens) and drops to an even 30 fps on devices that struggle. The weather tint also costs much less to draw.
Enemy trait badges in battle described your team’s tier instead of theirs.
Weeklies now open at midnight UTC for everyone, instead of at each player’s local midnight.
During a run that turns Mega abilities off, the Mega Stones collection showed the wrong ability, or none, for many Megas. It now shows the real one, marked “Off in this run”.
Tooltips near the edge of the screen are no longer cut off.
The Ice trait description was wrong in German, Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese. It now matches what the trait does.
v3.4.1
Endless followup: reach true infinity in normal mode, reach new heights in hardcore mode
2026-10-03
ENDLESS: NORMAL AND HARDCORE
The Endless card now opens its own menu, with a new look to match SpireMon. There are two modes, and each keeps its own run, its own “Continue · map N” and “↻ New run” buttons, and its own personal best. Switching between them, or playing anything else in between, loses neither run.
Normal: Endless 2.0’s balance is back, with its bosses, team compositions and level curve, so a strong enough team can climb forever. Every 5 maps there is an Elite Four and Champion, followed by a run-buff pick. You earn 100₽ per map, +20 for each Elite Four you have beaten (up to 200₽). Trade nodes, Underground portals and run buffs return, “?” nodes stop after the second Elite Four, and Mew still unlocks at map 25.
Normal: bosses now carry the name of the story trainer whose sprite they use (Drake, Jasmine and the rest). Mutants show up on both sides: you can find them among catch options from map 26 and at Mutant nodes from map 11, and enemy aces start turning Mutant from the map-10 Champion on.
Hardcore: the 3.4 Endless, now always on the full Challenge curve. The Creative choice is no longer offered; a run that already picked Creative keeps it.
Hardcore: the Mutate stat reward is no longer capped at +50%. Anything past 50% is kept, and once a Pokémon is fully Mutant the reward becomes “Augment”, a stat boost that stacks without limit.
YOUR RUNS FOLLOW YOU
Endless Normal, Endless Hardcore and SpireMon Endless runs now sync across your devices. The newest copy wins, and a run that has ended can’t come back from an older device. A run picked up from another device waits under “Continue” on its mode, without touching whatever else you are playing.
Endless runs (Endless Normal, Endless Hardcore and SpireMon Endless) play in one tab at a time: opening the same Endless run in a second tab asks “This run is open in another tab — continue here?”, and the old tab stops so it can’t overwrite your progress. Every other run never asks: open a second tab and start or check anything, like the Battle Tower while your story run is open, without overwriting the other tab’s run. Reloading never asks.
NEW
Misclick protection (Settings → Gameplay, off by default): the game asks before you release or trade away a team member, walk past a shiny or legendary, take a reward or passive card, teach a TM, or use a Griseous Orb, Rare Candy, Moon Stone or TM. Clicks, taps and hotkeys all go through it.
SpireMon: “Open 10” and “Open all” open many booster packs at once, with one summary of your new cards, shinies and duplicates. Tokens refunded for duplicates keep buying packs until you run out.
The Amulet Coin is now an item in every run mode: 3₽ for every battle won while you carry it, up to 150₽ per run.
The Soul Link weekly is back with an eased level curve: wild Pokémon, trainers, Gym Leaders and the Elite Four are lower level, so linked pairs have a real shot. The Randomizer layer is no longer locked behind the Co-op layer.
FIXES
Perpetual Motion (5,000₽), Truly Endless (25,000₽) and Unbroken (15,000₽) now pay their reward. If you already unlocked them, claim it in the achievements screen.
Soul Link runs no longer unlock story achievements.
A finished Soul Link room no longer follows you into later runs. It could freeze Endless or the Battle Tower at the first gym, turn Home into “Quit Soul Link” and restart a Soul Link run over yours.
Palkia’s Spacial Rend and Giratina’s Shadow Force no longer freeze the map when used mid-battle.
A lost fight could be fought again by reloading the page or going Home before pressing “Continue...”. A lost fight now always ends the run; Roar of Time and Escape Rope rescues still work.
A Griseous Orb can no longer be wasted on a Mutant that has no type left to reroll into.
SpireMon: “rung” now reads “run”.
v3.4
Endless Rework
2026-09-29
CREATIVE OR CHALLENGE
After your first Elite Four and Champion in Endless Mode, you choose how the run goes on. You choose once, and it holds for the rest of the run.
Creative Mode: enemy levels stop speeding up. From map 10 on they climb by at most 12 levels a map, and enemy trainers’ extra trait levels stop growing. A strong enough team can keep pace forever, but most runs still fall somewhere. You get 75% of the rewards (the ₽ per map and the numbers on the Champion rewards), and Creative keeps its own personal best on the Endless card.
Challenge Mode: the level curve keeps speeding up and you get full rewards. No team goes on forever here. Your mode shows next to your passive items.
New achievements: Perpetual Motion (map 100 in Creative, 5,000₽), Truly Endless (map 250 in Creative, 25,000₽) and Unbroken (map 30 in Challenge, 15,000₽).
✦ SIGNATURE ITEMS
Every evolution line in the game now has its own Signature Item: all 366 of them, legendaries included. Thick Club for Cubone, Leek for Farfetch’d, Amulet Coin for Meowth, Light Ball for Pikachu, Soul Dew for Latias and Latios, the Orbs for Dialga, Palkia and Giratina, and hundreds more, each made for its Pokémon.
A Signature Item powers up one Pokémon of its line. Depending on the Pokémon it can raise stats, add a chance to burn or paralyse, let it dodge or strike twice, survive a lethal hit, count as an extra type for traits, and more.
They are very rare. Now and then, one of the three passive items after a boss is a Signature Item instead, and sometimes one turns up among the Champion rewards. They are only offered for a Pokémon in your party.
You hold one at a time, outside your passive slots, and a new one replaces the old one. If its Pokémon leaves your party, the item stays with you but does nothing.
NO MORE INFINITE RUNS
Endless is meant to end. We hunted down every build that stayed just as strong however high enemy levels climbed, and fixed each one. Effects that dealt a share of the enemy’s max HP now fade when you are outlevelled, the same way attacks do. At equal level they work as before.
Ice trait: shattering a frozen enemy no longer deals a share of its max HP. Instead, the hit that shatters it deals 10% more per trait tier. Freeze chance is capped at 60%, and a Pokémon that just thawed can’t be frozen again before its next turn. Icy Rock refreezes at half the chance.
Poison stacks cap at 12. Poison and burn damage fades when whoever inflicted it is a lower level than its target.
The Steel trait and Metal Coat reflect only the damage that actually landed, and at most half the defender’s max HP per hit.
Pecha Berry, Leaf Stone, Spooky Plate, the Ghost trait’s heal and Mega Venusaur each heal at most 25% of the healer’s own max HP at once, and fade when outlevelled. So does Rocky Helmet’s damage.
Lum Berry raises the stat-stage cap to +20 instead of removing it. Protector and Coba Berry give at most +100% damage, and Mega Blaziken’s banked Attack is capped at +100.
A Pokémon that just lost a turn to flinching or sleep can’t be flinched on its next turn.
Light Clay secretly capped every hit you took at 200 damage. That was a leftover bug, and it is gone: Light Clay now does only what it says.
Gen 3–5 abilities in the weekly challenges: Ember Fever, Pilfer, Rough Skin and weather damage fade when outlevelled, reflecting abilities only reflect the damage that landed, and Living Wall works once per fight.
Mega Banette’s Vengeful Spirit now works once per fight, and Chople Berry revives once per fight. A Fighting-type Mutant Banette revived by Chople Berry took down every enemy in turn, at any level.
Bonuses for defeating an enemy (Chople Berry, HP Up, Kasib Berry and the rest) now go to the Pokémon that actually made the KO, instead of whichever Pokémon was in front.
v3.3
SpireMon Endless
2026-09-25
SPIREMON IS HERE TO STAY
SpireMon leaves the weekly rotation and becomes a permanent mode with its own card on the Challenge screen. Everything you earned while it was the weekly carries over: your Intro and Challenge clears, Celebi, your collection, tokens and missions.
SPIREMON ENDLESS IS OPEN
The five maps loop forever, with four fresh gym leaders and the Champion every lap. Endless pays 200₽ a map, counts as cleared at map 10 (10,000₽ the first time, 2,500₽ after), and your best run is shown on the SpireMon card.
Every lap is harder than the last. Enemies climb 2 more levels and then 3% more on top. Every second lap, each trainer and gym leader brings one more Pokémon (wild ones every fourth). From the fourth lap, every boss also fights under a second leader’s signature rule, and from the ninth lap under a third. Hover a boss on the map to see all of it.
Sudden Death: an Endless fight has a clock. From turn 12 (one turn earlier every second lap, never before turn 7) the end of every turn takes 25% of each fielded Pokémon’s max HP, then 50%, 75% and 100%. It lands after all healing and goes through Sturdy and Focus Sash, so even a wall that nothing can kill has to win in time. A badge on the field counts it down.
SUMMITS: A SPECIAL BOSS EVERY 50 MAPS
Every 50th map of SpireMon Endless ends in a summit instead of the Champion, in rotation: Red on Mt. Silver (map 50), Mewtwo in Cerulean Cave (map 100) and the Legendary Birds (map 150), then Red again.
Red: his Pikachu’s hits arc on to a second Pokémon, the blizzard takes a stage of Speed from each of your fielded Pokémon every turn, and his last two Pokémon rally once with full HP and +2 Attack.
Mewtwo: six times its HP, and its Psystrike hits every one of your lanes. Every third turn its Barrier stops all damage, and clones of your own strongest cards fight beside it.
The Legendary Birds fly in together with nearly four times their HP. Articuno’s hits cost Speed, Zapdos’ hits arc to every lane, Moltres burns your field, and when one falls the others heal and grow stronger.
A summit pays 5,000₽ and 5 extra Booster Tokens, and its relic offer brings the relics that give you a special Pokémon.
16 NEW RELICS
Signature relics help everyone and one Pokémon line a lot: Light Ball (Electric +20%, Pikachu and Raichu double), Thick Club (Ground +20%, Cubone and Marowak double), Leek (+10% crit chance, Farfetch’d always crits), Metal Powder (less damage on the turn a Pokémon enters, Ditto takes half), Poké Flute (enemies sleep on turn 1, Snorlax wakes up stronger) and Moon Stone (a stat when played; Clefairy, Jigglypuff and both Nidoran enter fully evolved).
Four relics bring a special Mythic Pokémon into your run, outside your eight slots: Helix Fossil (Lord Helix: every ally gains a stat each turn), Dome Fossil (Dome Kabutops: slashes every enemy), Old Amber (Ancient Aerodactyl: swoops on the healthiest enemy before every turn) and Silph Scope (the Ghost of Lavender Town: untouchable on the turn it appears, and frightens every enemy).
Six more bend the rules: Amulet Coin (more ₽ and tokens per gym), Rare Candy (levels for your two weakest cards after every gym), Master Ball (a fourth catch card, always Epic or better), King’s Rock (your hits can make foes flinch), Metronome (damage grows the longer a Pokémon stays on the field) and Expert Belt (super-effective hits +30%).
CARD BALANCE
Several cards were rarely worth a slot, many of them Epics. They have been reworked or made cheaper, and one early-game standout was trimmed.
Jynx costs 3 (was 4) and freezes the enemy in front when played. It still costs 1 less for every card you played that turn.

HOW A FIGHT WORKS
A fight is three lanes a side. Each turn you get 3 Mana and draw back up to three cards, and most cards cost 1 to 3. Play a card into a lane: it attacks once as it enters, then its ability fires. At the end of every turn, everything on the field attacks in Speed order, fastest first, each at the enemy in its own lane.
The enemy fields three Pokémon at a time and refills empty lanes, but an enemy never attacks the moment it comes in: its first swing is at the end of the turn. Hold a card over a lane to preview every hit it would land. Hover or tap a Pokémon to read its ability; abilities announce themselves when they fire.

NEW WEEKLY CHALLENGE: CATCH THE SPIRE
